What this role does

As a provider (demo: Meera Iyer or Arjun Rao) you complete onboarding, connect a PayPal business account, negotiate and sign contracts, submit evidence for each milestone, and follow your payouts.

Signing in

Open the demo, choose Try the live demo (or go to /login) and pick Meera Iyer under “Explore the live demo”. No password is needed in the sandbox. You land on /provider.

Step by step

1. Read your overview

What to deliver next, what is waiting on clients, and what has been paid.

  1. Open Overview.
  2. The tiles show what is paid to you, payouts pending, and what is held in PayPal for your milestones.
  3. Deliver next lists funded milestones that are ready for evidence, with Submit evidence.

2. Complete onboarding and connect PayPal

Operations validates providers before payouts are switched on.

  1. Open Onboarding & PayPal.
  2. Fill in business details. The tax id is stored masked to its last four characters plus a one-way fingerprint.
  3. Under PayPal business account, enter the e-mail of your PayPal (Sandbox) business account; releases are captured to it.
  4. Add licences or registration documents (PDF, PNG or JPEG, up to 10 MB). PaySettle keeps the name, size and SHA-256 fingerprint.
  5. Wait for Validated by Ops. Your account security (authenticator app, sessions, password) is on Account & security.

3. Negotiate and sign contracts

Clients invite you; you shape the contract with them in the negotiation room.

  1. Open Contracts and choose a contract.
  2. Prune optional milestones or clauses, append custom milestones, adjust days and shares. Every change is a new revision and voids earlier signatures.
  3. When the policy gate passes, sign the same revision the client signed. The contract locks (or goes to Operations if the risk score is 75 or more).

4. Submit evidence for a milestone

Evidence opens once the tranche is held in PayPal and the milestones it depends on are settled.

  1. From Deliver next, press Submit evidence.
  2. Upload exactly what the contract names (for example 1 link + 1 PDF). The live checklist shows what is still missing.
  3. Files are hashed into a root hash; signals such as counts, file types, photo GPS distance and dates are computed. EXIF data is a hint, not proof.
  4. The client has 48 hours to review. If they request a fix, the page lists the items and you have 72 hours; you have three attempts in total.

5. Follow payouts

Each approved milestone is captured to your PayPal business account, minus the platform fee.

  1. Open Payouts.
  2. Per tranche you see the authorization, what is still held, the hold window, and each release with gross, fee, what you receive and the PayPal reference.

6. Respond to a dispute

Only the disputed milestone is frozen.

  1. Open Disputes and the case.
  2. Make sealed offers in Tier 1; add your statement and files to the vault in Tier 2; a mediator decides in Tier 3 if needed.

What the money states mean

PaySettle never keeps the client's money in its own account. Each tranche (a block of milestones that fits inside one PayPal hold window) is an authorization on the client's PayPal account; each approved milestone is a partial capture from it.

You seeWhere the money is
Planned — not fundedNowhere yet. The contract is signed and locked; the client has not approved the PayPal hold.
Waiting for PayPal approvalThe order exists in PayPal; the client still has to approve it there. Nothing is reserved until they do.
Held in PayPalReserved on the client's PayPal account (an authorization). Not paid to anyone yet.
Partly releasedSome milestones were approved and captured to the provider; the rest is still held.
ReleasedThat milestone's amount was captured to the provider's PayPal account, minus the platform fee (2.5% per release in the demo).
Frozen: in disputeOnly the disputed milestone's amount is frozen; it stays held in PayPal while the dispute runs. Other milestones carry on.
Returned to clientReleased back to the client: the unused part of the hold is voided, or a captured amount is refunded after a verdict.
Rolled overThe hold window was closing, so the unfinished remainder moved into a new tranche that the client funds again.
Settling / ClosedThe hold window is ending: approved work is paid and unstarted milestones go back to the client. Closed means nothing is left on that tranche.
Paused for checksReconciliation found a difference between the contract, the ledger and PayPal. Releases on that tranche wait until Finance resolves it.

Limits of this demo

  • Sandbox only. Money is either PayPal Sandbox money or simulated by the mock rail (“Simulated PayPal (mock)”). Nothing real moves, and the yellow banner at the top of every app page says so.
  • The mock rail keeps its records in memory. After a server restart, Finance's reconciliation may show older tranches as “no PayPal data”; the ledger is still checked against the contract.
  • AI is advisory. The AI verifier, the mediation proposal and the drafting help only suggest. People approve, sign and decide; the rule-based checks run with or without AI. Without an AI key the panels say “AI unavailable”.
  • Time can be simulated. The Judge Playground moves a session's virtual clock only; deadlines you see on seeded data use each contract's own clock.
  • Demo people are shared. Everyone using the demo signs in as the same personas, and Playground scenarios add contracts to them, so lists grow over time.
  • PaySettle is a hackathon build, not a licensed payment, escrow or legal service. Contract text and dispute outcomes are not legal advice.
