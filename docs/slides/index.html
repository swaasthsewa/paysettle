How it was conceptualised

Three things that happened to me, and one missing sentence

1 · What happened

A trip paid in advance. The hotel at check-in was not the one booked, and the agency said the hotel decides. The money had already gone.

A client who paid late. The work was signed off; the last payment took months of reminders.

A project that kept growing. "Small" changes, none written down, all expected free, same deadline.

2 · One insight

Each had a contract.
None had a definition of done.

Nobody wrote down, in a way anyone could check, what finished meant, what a change costs, or when the money moves.

3 · Not just me
85%
of freelancers are paid late at least some of the time
15%
are always paid on time
25%
of employers had freelancers miss deadlines
Remote, The State of Freelance Work 2025: 3,300 freelancers in 10 countries and 1,900 employers (pp. 3, 4, 16, 21)
So the design protects both sides, with four rules set before the first line of code
Agreement first, money second
No hold until both sign the same revision.
AI advises, people decide
No AI output can sign, capture or book money.
One milestone, one blast radius
A dispute freezes only its own share.
Every change is signed
A change order, priced and held, or nothing changes.

Feature · the criteria compiler

Vague words in. Checkable criteria out.

Before anyone signs, every acceptance criterion must be something a third person could check.

Rejected: nobody can check it
  • "The website should be fast and modern."
  • "User-friendly dashboard for the operations team."
  • "High quality code."
  • "Kitchen cabinets look nice and are well finished."
  • "Make changes as needed until the client is happy."
Accepted: anyone can check it
  • Lighthouse mobile score 85 or more on staging, report attached.
  • CI green on main with all 214 unit tests passing.
  • Client signs the UAT sign-off sheet for all 12 scripts.
  • Cabinets installed per drawing K-12; 6 dated photos, one per wall.
  • API p95 under 300 ms at 200 users, load-test report attached.

Can it be cheated?

Fool the AI. The money still doesn’t move.

The AI only advises. Money reaches PayPal through signed decisions by people, never through the AI’s answer.

1

AI advises

Reads the evidence against the criteria. Can be fooled.

If fooled: wrong advice, nothing else. Party text is marked untrusted; 0 of 12 hidden instructions followed.
2

Rule checks

File fingerprints, reused-photo match, dates and places, signatures.

Not open to persuasion: no text in the evidence changes a rule.
3

The client approves

With an authenticator code, signed over the evidence fingerprint.

An agent’s approve_release only waits for this. By default, silence goes to Operations, not to payment.
4

Both sides make sealed offers

If they disagree. Within 5 points, it settles at the midpoint.

Offers stay hidden until both are in. Only that milestone is frozen.
5

A human mediator signs

From the sealed evidence file; an AI proposal is advisory.

Conflict check; a second mediator above $5,000.
$

PayPal captures

Verified webhooks, one capture per retry, ledger rows that cannot be edited.

18 cheat attempts run as tests on every push. Money moved: $0.
Where it can still be fooled: a well-forged photo can fool a person too. The mediator and the evidence vault are the backstop; signed camera photos (C2PA) and independent inspections are next.

When to use it, and when not to

The right tool for staged work with provable stages

Use PaySettle when
  • ✓Work is delivered in stages over weeks or months: software, consulting, fit-outs, events, travel.
  • ✓Each stage can be proved: a URL, a test report, a signed sheet, dated photos, an inspection certificate.
  • ✓The two sides haven't worked together, or work from different places.
  • ✓One disagreement shouldn't stop the whole project.
  • ✓Work continues month to month and changes along the way.
Choose something else when
  • ✕It's a one-off purchase or an instant digital good: a normal checkout is simpler.
  • ✕"Done" is pure taste and nothing in it can be checked.
  • ✕The deal legally needs a licensed escrow agent, such as property.
  • ✕It's hourly work billed on timesheets, with no deliverable per stage.
  • ✕You need to move real money today: PaySettle runs on PayPal Sandbox only.

Why this is needed next

Work is changing. Payment terms have to catch up.

1

AI makes work fast to produce, and hard to judge

When a deliverable takes an hour to generate, "looks fine" stops being proof.

→ Criteria anyone can check, with proof attached.
2

Agents will buy and sell services

Software agents will draft, negotiate and track work for people and businesses.

→ Contracts machines can read, with spend limits people set.
3

Work happens between strangers, across borders

Trust can't rest on knowing each other or sharing a court.

→ Money held by PayPal, released by rules both signed.
4

Work is continuous, not one-off

Retainers and long projects change month to month.

→ Change orders and contracts that roll into the next period.
5

Every payment must explain itself

Finance teams and auditors expect a trail from promise to payment.

→ Evidence hash, ledger and PayPal, reconciled to the cent.
→

The rails are ready

PayPal already offers authorizations, partial captures and verified webhooks. What was missing is the definition of done.

PaySettle adds that layer.
